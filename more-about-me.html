Hello!

Hey, I’m Kai. I’m a 23-year-old game developer and environment artist with a background in Computer Science. I love creating immersive worlds, building systems from scratch, and bringing ideas to life inside Unreal Engine.

Ever since I was little, my brother and I have been making video games together. We started in Unity and eventually moved to Unreal Engine. I'm an artist at heart, and I love taking the ideas in my head and turning them into something real—that's what keeps me passionate about game development.

My projects range from replicated multiplayer dungeon crawlers to narrative-driven solo horror experiences. I create all of my assets and environments from scratch, giving each project its own visual identity.

I'm always looking to grow, learn more, refine my skills, and create better, higher-quality projects.

About Me

The two things I think will help me live a fulfilling life is one art, and two helping people. I believe that art is a necessity for everyone, and almost everyone finds art in their lives. I have a loose definition of art. To me art is having an original idea and somehow manifesting it into reality. I do visual arts, but I mainly make games, which is an art. There’s some universe, story or narrative I want to create and I can make it through code. I can model the assets I want, sound design my scenes, set up lighting and ambience. Creating games is such a freeing form of art and it’s the whole reason I got into computer science in the first place.
Secondly I want to help people. Whether it’s through teaching, donating, volunteering, I think it’s important to live a fulfilling life.
On my free time when I’m not working on any projects I like to go to the gym, or to play sports. I played volleyball in college, and I often play basketball in the park with my friends.
I love animals, I had two cats named Mas and Menos (twins from the show Teen Titans), and a dog named Hazel.
During my junior year of college I studied abroad in Japan. That was the furthest I’ve ever been from home for the longest amount of time, and it completely opened up my perspective of the world and life. Before my exchange year I did a program called woofing, which is basically a work exchange. It allowed me to visit and live in different parts of the country side. Being out in rural Japan all the way across the world made me realize how big the world was. I realized how many different ways there was to live, and it made my problems feel trivial. It shaped my perspective. Since then I’ve wanted to do more woofing or worldpackers type experiences. I love culture, languages and traveling and I think it’s hugely important.
